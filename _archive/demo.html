Tell it what you're after — it figures out the rest

No knobs, no weights to tune. Just answer like you would to a friend: what you're craving, veg or non-veg, how far you'll go, and what mood you're in. The system decides on its own how much to trust peer ratings vs. your taste profile vs. review sentiment, and explains its pick.

Illustrative only — runs on a small hardcoded sample menu in your browser, not the trained models or datasets.

Under the hood

What's happening when you change an answer

The questions above are deliberately non-technical, but each one maps onto a real part of the hybrid pipeline described in the project breakdown.

Cuisine, veg/non-veg & distance

These act as hard filters and feed the content-based signal — the same cuisine, price, and location similarity the real system computes against restaurants a user already liked. Distance also applies a small proximity boost so "near me" genuinely matters without overriding quality.

Your mood

Mood is the stand-in for personalized fusion weights. Picking "something familiar" leans the blend toward collaborative filtering; "guaranteed good experience" leans it toward review sentiment; "adventurous" leans toward taste-profile match. In the real system these weights are learned per user via NNLS regression rather than chosen from a menu.

The match percentage

That's the fused score — the weighted combination of the three signals plus the proximity boost. It's the single number the final ranking sorts on.

The green sentence

That's the explainability layer, the project's second original contribution. Every pick states its reasoning in plain English rather than handing you a bare score.
